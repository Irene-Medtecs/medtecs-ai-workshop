STEP 02LEARN1 min

I don’t know how to write a prompt

Watch Irene act like a beginner. You do not need to memorize a special formula before you ask for help.

MESSAGE 1 · The deliberately simple question
I'm organizing an AI workshop for our employees. I need to divide them into groups for their final presentations, but I have never done this with AI. Can you help me?
Open ChatGPT or Gemini. Type the question exactly as you would ask a colleague. Read the answer; do not expect the same wording every time.
For Irene · facilitator notes

Say: “I will pretend I have never used AI. I know my problem, but not how to write a prompt.” Let the reply appear live.

STEP 03WATCH2 min

Ask AI what it needs

When you do not know what to put in a prompt, ask AI to guide you. Answer one question at a time; say “I don’t know” when needed.

MESSAGE 2 · Request clarifying questions
I have a Google Sheet with the participants' survey responses, but I'm not sure what information you need. Please ask me questions one at a time so we can work this out together.
Questions may cover participant count, number of teams, mix of departments, AI familiarity and whether any people must be placed together or apart.
Answer each relevant question using confirmed information. Say “I don’t know yet” rather than inventing an answer.
For Irene · facilitator notes

Ask the room: “Did I need to know how to write a perfect prompt to get here?”

STEP 04LEARN2 min

Give AI the survey

Use a cleaned survey file. Exclude Ever’s first test response, and share only approved fields.

MESSAGE 3 · Introduce your source
I have a spreadsheet containing our pre-workshop survey responses. Use it to help me form balanced teams for the final presentation. Important: the FIRST survey response was a test submitted by Ever. Exclude that response from all analysis and participant counts. I will upload the spreadsheet now.
Upload the prepared file or paste the approved survey fields. Do not project personal details.
For Irene · facilitator notes

Prepare an anonymized copy in advance. The connected spreadsheet could not be read from this chat, so this site contains no survey rows or real team assignments.

Before projecting: use participant IDs; remove email addresses and unnecessary personal data. Download the approved sheet in advance. Do not assume a Sheets URL grants AI access.

Count valid participants after removing the first test response. Do not assume a fixed total.
STEP 05LEARN2 min

Let AI write the prompt

By now AI has your goal and context. Let it organize your instructions into a reusable prompt.

MESSAGE 4 · Ask for a better prompt
Based on everything we have discussed, write a clear prompt to create balanced teams for our final-day presentation. Include the survey as the source, the exclusion of Ever's first test response, balancing criteria, the output table, and the checks we should perform. If I have not specified the number of teams or participants per team, ask me before creating the final prompt. Explain the instructions in beginner-friendly language.
What just changed? The first message only explained the problem. The improved prompt now describes the information, rules, output and checks.
For Irene · facilitator notes

Compare the generated prompt with the first one-sentence request. Point out that clear instructions can emerge through conversation.

STEP 06WATCH2 min

Run the grouping prompt

You have worked together with AI to write the instructions. Now put them to work.

MESSAGE 5 · Propose groups
Use the prompt we developed and the uploaded survey. First remove Ever's first test response and count the valid entries. Using the team count or group size that I have confirmed, propose reasonably equal-sized teams with a mix of departments, relevant work skills and AI experience. Show Team, Participant ID, Department, AI familiarity and a short grouping rationale. Use only fields actually present in the survey. If a balancing factor is absent, say so and use available fields. Do not invent missing values.
If the questionnaire does not include a requested balancing field, AI must say so and use only the available data.
Project the proposed grouping, preferably using participant IDs. Ask the class what they would check before accepting it.
STEP 07CHECK8 min

Check the teams before announcing them

AI suggests teams. You verify the count, coverage and balance before announcing them.

MESSAGE 6 · Audit and revise
Audit the proposed grouping against the source spreadsheet. Confirm that Ever's FIRST test entry is excluded (not every submission by Ever, if there are later legitimate entries); count the valid participants; list any omitted or duplicated participant IDs; calculate the team sizes; identify imbalances in departments or AI familiarity; and propose a revised grouping if needed. If you cannot verify a point against the spreadsheet, say so.

STEP 08LEARN3 min

You just learned how to prompt

You started by explaining a problem—not by knowing any technical vocabulary. These ingredients emerged during your conversation.

01 · CONTEXT

What is happening?

Our team presentation.

02 · GOAL

What must happen?

Form balanced teams.

03 · INPUT

What can AI use?

Valid survey responses.

04 · RULES

What matters?

Exclude the test and balance skills.

05 · OUTPUT

What should it show?

A readable team table.

06 · CHECK

How do we trust it?

Verify counts and assignments.

Reusable opening line: “I know what I need to do, but I don’t know how to write the prompt. Ask me the questions you need.”
STEP 09WATCH10 min

Gemini · Philippine Army BDU Contract Review

A real work example: use Gemini to review the approved Philippine Army BDU contract package, but require every important finding to point back to the source.

DEMO PROMPT · SOURCE-FIRST REVIEW
Review the Philippine Army BDU contract documents I provide.

Use ONLY the uploaded documents.

Create a concise management review covering:
1. What the procurement/project is
2. Relevant lot, quantity and contract/budget figures stated in the documents
3. Key delivery and performance obligations
4. Penalty / liquidated damages / inspection / termination / performance-security provisions that are expressly supported
5. Important risks, open questions or items requiring management attention

For every important finding, identify the source document and exact clause/page when available.

Do not invent missing terms, amounts, dates or legal conclusions. If something is unclear or conflicting, label it: NEEDS VERIFICATION.
Upload the approved BDU source documents. Ask Gemini to review them, then open at least one cited source and verify it live.
STEP 10CHECK10 min

A confident answer still needs evidence.

The value of the demo is not just the summary—it is learning how to challenge the result before using it.

Source?

Can we find the statement in the uploaded document?

Exact?

Do the page, clause, amount and obligation match?

Missing?

Did Gemini label unclear or unsupported points instead of guessing?

FOLLOW-UP · AUDIT YOUR OWN ANSWER
Audit your previous answer against the uploaded source documents.

For each material statement, confirm whether it is directly supported. If supported, show the source document and page/clause. If not fully supported, correct it or label it NEEDS VERIFICATION.

Do not add new facts.
Takeaway: AI can accelerate review. The person using the result still verifies the source.

Create a manager-ready one-page visual.

Use the same approved sources, but change the output for a manager or decision-maker.

DEMO PROMPT · MANAGER-READY VISUAL
Create a professional one-page executive infographic for a manager or decision-maker reviewing this Philippine Army BDU contract matter.

Use only the uploaded source documents.

Focus on:
- what the project/bidding is
- relevant lot, quantity and contract/budget figures stated
- key delivery/performance obligations
- penalty/liquidated damages/inspection/termination/performance-security issues expressly supported

STEP 13DO20 min

Find repetitive work in your own Gmail.

Now everyone uses Gemini to discover 3–5 recurring workflows from their own last 30 days of work email. Gmail stays read-only.

PROMPT 1 · FIND MY WORKFLOWS
Review my work Gmail from the last 30 days and help me identify repetitive work that I personally do that could potentially be assisted or automated with AI.

GMAIL IS READ-ONLY.
Do not send, draft, reply to, forward, delete, archive, label, move, or modify anything.

Base your findings only on recurring patterns you can actually observe in my emails.

Look for patterns such as:
- similar emails I repeatedly draft or reply to
- information I repeatedly collect, organize, or summarize
- recurring follow-ups or reminders
- document, quotation, proposal, or version comparisons
- recurring status updates or reports
- information I repeatedly copy between emails, documents, spreadsheets, or systems
- approval, review, confirmation, or sign-off processes
- recurring requests where I gather information from several people
- recurring processes that follow similar steps each time

Identify 3–5 real workflow opportunities.

For each workflow, show:
1. Recurring task
2. What triggers it
3. What I currently do
4. What AI could help with
5. What still needs human review

Only identify work that appears to be performed by me. Do not invent tasks, frequency, responsibilities, or workflows.

Do not reproduce confidential email content, names, contract details, financial figures, personal information, passwords, credentials, or full email text unnecessarily. Summarize the workflow pattern rather than the confidential content.

Finally, recommend ONE workflow that would be practical to improve first. Prefer something repetitive, useful, clearly supported by my Gmail, small enough to test, and realistic to improve with AI or automation. Explain briefly why you selected it.
Privacy: The result should describe the workflow pattern—not expose confidential email content or other people’s information.

- Size

- Ice level

- a clear Submit Order button
- a confirmation after submission

No notes. No login. No payment.
