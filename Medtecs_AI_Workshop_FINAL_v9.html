STEP 04WATCH3 min

Now give AI the real source and the rules.

Upload the survey, explain the one test response to exclude, and tell AI what a usable team looks like. The prompt below is designed to produce teams you can actually use for the final project.

MESSAGE 3 · Upload survey + form the teams
I have uploaded our pre-workshop survey responses. Please use ONLY this spreadsheet to form the teams for our final group project.

Important data rule:
- The FIRST survey response was a test submitted by Ever. Exclude that first test response from all counts and grouping.
- Keep every other valid participant.
- Do not invent missing information.

Before grouping:
1. Count the total survey responses.
2. Show which one response you excluded as the test.
3. State the final valid participant count.

Then form practical teams:
- Aim for 5–6 people per team and do not create a team larger than 6 unless you ask me first.
- PRIMARY factor: group people with compatible/shared work problems, use cases or project interests so they can build one final project together.
- SECONDARY factors: department/work function and AI experience. Where practical, avoid putting all experienced AI users in one team.
- Use only fields that actually exist in the survey.

Output:
A. Validation summary: total responses, excluded test response, valid participant count.
B. Final team table: Team | Members | Department/Function | Shared work problem / possible project direction.
C. Audit: team sizes, total people assigned, duplicates, omissions.

IMPORTANT: The total number of people assigned across all teams MUST equal the valid participant count, and every valid participant must appear exactly once. If your audit does not match, fix the grouping BEFORE showing me the final answer.
Upload the spreadsheet, paste this prompt, and run it. Do not announce the teams yet—first inspect the validation and audit.
Presenter notes

Use the real survey but avoid projecting unnecessary personal information such as email addresses. The shared work problem is the primary grouping factor because each team needs one project to work on together.

STEP 05WATCH3 min

The answer can look good and still be wrong.

Read the validation first, then the team table, then the audit. Do not judge the result only by how polished it looks.

1 · COUNT

Valid participants

Does the number match the spreadsheet after excluding only the first test response?

2 · TEAMS

Practical sizes

Are teams around 5–6 people, with no oversized group?

3 · PURPOSE

Can they work together?

Do members have a compatible work problem or project direction?

What happened in our Gemini test: the answer stated 36 valid participants, but the proposed teams contained 45 names in total, and one team had 12 people. The formatting looked professional, but the math did not match.
Ask the class: “Would you use these teams immediately?” Then count before accepting the result.

STEP 07CHECK3 min

If something is wrong, ask AI to audit and repair it.

Do not manually start over. Tell AI exactly what to verify, then require a corrected final table.

MESSAGE 4 · Audit + repair
Before I use these teams, audit your answer against the uploaded spreadsheet.

Please verify all of the following:
1. Ever's FIRST test response is excluded and no other valid participant is excluded.
2. Recalculate the valid participant count directly from the spreadsheet.
3. Count the members in every proposed team and show the total assigned.
4. Check that every valid participant appears exactly once.
5. Identify any duplicate or missing participant.
6. Confirm that no team has more than 6 people.
7. Confirm that each team has a reasonable shared work problem / project direction based on the survey.

If ANY check fails, rebuild the teams and run the audit again before giving me the final answer.

Return ONLY:
- Valid participant count
- Corrected final team table: Team | Members | Department/Function | Shared work problem / project direction
- Team sizes
- Final audit: total assigned, duplicates, omissions

Do not say the audit passed unless the valid count, total assigned and unique participants all match.
Run the audit. Compare the corrected result with the spreadsheet. Once the numbers match and the groups are practical, announce the teams.
AI can help repair its own output, but your final verification is still against the original survey.

STEP 10CHECK10 min

A neat table can still be wrong.

Stay in the same 4A demo. Ask a real question of the table, then check the arithmetic and missing information.

4A FOLLOW-UP · 300,000 pieces
We need 300,000 pieces delivered to Manila.

1. Which suppliers can actually take this order? Say why for each.
2. For the ones that can, show the product-cost arithmetic using the quoted figures.
3. Calculate a comparable landed cost only if all necessary freight, delivery and other relevant costs are known.
4. If a supplier cannot be compared fairly because something is missing, say so instead of estimating.

Do not assume FOB, EXW and CIF prices are directly comparable.

MOQ

Does 300,000 meet each quoted minimum?

Comparable?

FOB, EXW and CIF are not automatically equivalent.

Missing?

What must be clarified before a purchasing decision?

Expected check from the 4A sample: Greenfield’s quoted MOQ is 500,000, so a 300,000-piece order does not meet that minimum. Do not claim a lowest landed cost unless the necessary delivery/freight inputs are comparable.

DEMO · Find repetitive work from Gmail
Review my work Gmail from the last 30 days and help me identify repetitive work that I personally do and that could potentially be assisted or automated with AI.

Do not send, draft, delete, archive, label or modify anything. I only want analysis.

Look for recurring patterns such as:
- similar emails I repeatedly draft or reply to
- information I repeatedly collect or summarize
- recurring follow-ups or reminders
- document or quotation comparisons
- status updates or reports
- information I repeatedly copy between emails, documents or systems
- approval or review processes

Identify 3–5 real workflow opportunities based only on patterns you can actually see in my emails. Do not invent tasks or frequency.

For each one, show:
Recurring task | What triggers it | What I currently do | What AI could help with | What still needs human review

Do not reproduce confidential email content unnecessarily. Summarize the work pattern instead.

At the end, recommend one small, repetitive workflow that would be practical for me to improve with AI. Explain why.
Presenter: Run this first with an approved work account or a prepared/sanitized example. Show the class what a useful workflow opportunity looks like. You do not need to build it yet.
What to point out: A useful result is specific—something you actually do repeatedly—not a vague suggestion such as “automate email.”
