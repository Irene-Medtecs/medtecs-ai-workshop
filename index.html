STEP 02LEARN1 min

I don’t know how to write a prompt

Watch Irene act like a beginner. You do not need to memorize a special formula before you ask for help.

MESSAGE 1 · The deliberately simple question
I'm organizing an AI workshop for our employees. I need to divide them into groups for their final presentations, but I have never done this with AI. Can you help me?
Open ChatGPT or Gemini. Type the question exactly as you would ask a colleague. Read the answer; do not expect the same wording every time.
For Irene · facilitator notes

Say: “I will pretend I have never used AI. I know my problem, but not how to write a prompt.” Let the reply appear live.

STEP 03WATCH2 min

Ask AI what it needs

When you do not know what to put in a prompt, ask AI to guide you. Answer one question at a time; say “I don’t know” when needed.

MESSAGE 2 · Request clarifying questions
I have a Google Sheet with the participants' survey responses, but I'm not sure what information you need. Please ask me questions one at a time so we can work this out together.
Questions may cover participant count, number of teams, mix of departments, AI familiarity and whether any people must be placed together or apart.
Answer each relevant question using confirmed information. Say “I don’t know yet” rather than inventing an answer.
For Irene · facilitator notes

Ask the room: “Did I need to know how to write a perfect prompt to get here?”

STEP 04LEARN2 min

Give AI the survey

Give AI only the information it needs. Upload a cleaned, anonymized spreadsheet if file upload is available; otherwise paste the relevant columns. Check that the first test response has been excluded.

MESSAGE 3 · Introduce your source
I have a spreadsheet containing our pre-workshop survey responses. Use it to help me form balanced teams for the final presentation. Important: the FIRST survey response was a test submitted by Ever. Exclude that response from all analysis and participant counts. I will upload the spreadsheet now.
Download the Google Sheet as Excel (.xlsx), remove unnecessary personal identifiers for the projected demo, and upload the prepared file to your company-approved AI environment.
For Irene · facilitator notes

Prepare an anonymized copy in advance. The connected spreadsheet could not be read from this chat, so this site contains no survey rows or real team assignments.

Do not enter a fixed number of participants yet. Count the valid responses only after excluding Ever’s first test entry. The live Google Sheet is not embedded in this handout.
Before projecting: Replace names with participant IDs and remove email addresses and unnecessary personal data. Download the spreadsheet in advance. A Google Sheets URL alone may not grant the AI access; upload the approved file or paste its cleaned data instead. Do not upload survey data to an unapproved tool.
STEP 05LEARN2 min

Let AI write the prompt

By now AI has your goal and context. Let it organize your instructions into a reusable prompt.

MESSAGE 4 · Ask for a better prompt
Based on everything we have discussed, write a clear prompt to create balanced teams for our final-day presentation. Include the survey as the source, the exclusion of Ever's first test response, balancing criteria, the output table, and the checks we should perform. If I have not specified the number of teams or participants per team, ask me before creating the final prompt. Explain the instructions in beginner-friendly language.
What just changed? The first message only explained the problem. The improved prompt now describes the information, rules, output and checks.
For Irene · facilitator notes

Compare the generated prompt with the first one-sentence request. Point out that clear instructions can emerge through conversation.

STEP 06WATCH2 min

Run the grouping prompt

You have worked together with AI to write the instructions. Now put them to work.

MESSAGE 5 · Propose groups
Use the prompt we developed and the uploaded survey. First remove Ever's first test response and count the valid entries. Using the team count or group size that I have confirmed, propose reasonably equal-sized teams with a mix of departments, relevant work skills and AI experience. Show Team, Participant ID, Department, AI familiarity and a short grouping rationale. Use only fields actually present in the survey. If a balancing factor is absent, say so and use available fields. Do not invent missing values.
If the questionnaire does not include a requested balancing field, AI must say so and use only the available data.
Project the proposed grouping, preferably using participant IDs. Ask the class what they would check before accepting it.
STEP 07CHECK2 min

Check the teams before announcing them

Never mistake a polished table for a verified result. This is the part you still own.

MESSAGE 6 · Audit and revise
Audit the proposed grouping against the source spreadsheet. Confirm that Ever's FIRST test entry is excluded (not every submission by Ever, if there are later legitimate entries); count the valid participants; list any omitted or duplicated participant IDs; calculate the team sizes; identify imbalances in departments or AI familiarity; and propose a revised grouping if needed. If you cannot verify a point against the spreadsheet, say so.
CHECK 1

Did we remove the first test entry?

CHECK 2

Everyone exactly once?

CHECK 3

CHECK 4

Any practical changes needed?

Compare the final names and counts independently with the cleaned sheet before you announce teams. A second AI answer is not independent verification.

PROMPT · Standardize without guessing
Below are three supplier replies for the same item. Put them into one comparison table, one row per supplier. Columns: Supplier, Unit price, Currency, Incoterm, MOQ, Lead time, Payment terms, Validity, Notes. Keep the numbers exactly as written. If something is missing, write 'not stated'; do not guess. Here are the supplier replies:

[PASTE THE THREE REPLIES]
Copy both blocks and submit them together in one message. Check the three quoted unit prices: 0.042, 0.0395 and 0.0455.
What matters is not the visual polish of the table. Check every number and mark missing facts explicitly.
STEP 10LEARN8 min

Ask a real question of the table

Creating a table is preparation. Now ask the question the table is meant to help answer.

FOLLOW-UP · 300,000 pieces
We need 300,000 pieces delivered to Manila. Which quotations meet the stated minimum-order quantity for this quantity? Explain why. Supplier capacity and acceptance are not confirmed by MOQ alone. For those that can, show the arithmetic for quoted product cost. Calculate a comparable landed cost only if all necessary freight, delivery and other relevant costs are known. Do not assume FOB, EXW and CIF prices are directly comparable. Identify any missing details needed for a fair landed-cost comparison.
Check: Greenfield’s stated MOQ is 500,000, so a 300,000-piece order does not meet its quoted minimum. Do not claim that one supplier has the lowest landed cost without comparable delivery and freight figures.
For Irene · facilitator notes

Pause before the AI answer and ask the room whether the lowest unit price necessarily wins.

STEP 11CHECK8 min

Find what is missing and risky

One powerful follow-up works on quotations, tenders, inventory reports and many other workplace documents.

FOLLOW-UP · Find the gaps
Before issuing a purchase order, review the three quotations and list: (1) important information the suppliers did not state, (2) information that does not match across the quotations, and (3) terms that may create a risk or require clarification. Keep the list short. Quote the source detail for each finding. Do not invent missing facts.

What is missing?

Unstated cost, delivery or validity.

What does not match?

Different Incoterms and conditions.

What creates risk?

Payment, delivery and unclear terms.

AI flags questions to investigate. It does not approve suppliers or make a purchasing decision.
STEP 12TRY IT7 min

Get the table into Excel

Ask for a format Excel can easily receive. You do not have to manually rebuild the comparison.

FOLLOW-UP · Export
Give me the corrected comparison table again as tab-separated text with one header row, no Markdown, no bullets and no explanation. Preserve all verified numbers and missing-value labels.
Copy the response and paste into cell A1 of a blank Excel sheet. Confirm that each field appears in its own column.
If it pastes into one column: Use Excel Data → Text to Columns, choose Tab, and preview before applying.

- We need delivery before 20 October.
- Please quote freight separately.

PROMPT · Draft the message
Turn the five points below into a short professional email to a supplier. Include all five. Do not introduce new facts, commitments or dates. Add a clear subject line.

[PASTE THE FIVE POINTS]

For Irene · facilitator notes

STEP 14CHECK10 min

Change the tone; check the facts

Ask for a warmer, neutral or firmer tone, then verify that the underlying facts did not change.

FOLLOW-UP · Three versions
Rewrite the same email in three versions: warm and collaborative, neutral and professional, and firm but courteous. Keep the facts exactly the same. Show a short note explaining where each tone might be appropriate.
FOLLOW-UP · Audit the draft
Compare the final email to my five original notes. List any facts that were omitted, changed or invented. Check the requested delivery date and whether the email asks for freight separately.
Human check: Choose the appropriate tone for the relationship and approve the final email yourself.

STEP 16CHECK10 min

NOTEBOOKLM · Decision checklist

FOLLOW-UP · Deliberately missing detail
